1. Analog vs Digital—Technical Truths (and Why They Get So Messy)
Analog
Definition: “Analog” means continuous. The signal varies smoothly, with infinite possible values within a range.

Example in music: When you play a vinyl record, the needle moves through grooves that are wavy, continuous forms—literally a physical “analog” of the soundwaves.

Analog synths: These use circuits—resistors, capacitors, transistors—to shape and manipulate electrical voltages that continuously fluctuate. The pitch, timbre, and volume all exist as smooth, unbroken changes.

Digital
Definition: “Digital” means discrete. The signal jumps in steps—information is chopped into units (binary: 0 or 1, off or on). No in-between.

Example in music: CDs and MP3s store audio as a long sequence of numbers, each representing a tiny sample of the soundwave. The “continuous” waveform is approximated by lots of very small, discrete steps.

Digital synths: These use computer chips or microprocessors to process, store, and generate sound in those numerical steps.

But…Fingers Are Digital?!
Yes! “Digital” comes from digitus, Latin for “finger.” Counting on fingers = digital.

So when you use your fingers (“digits”) to play an “analog” synth, you are literally acting “digitally”—but the signal the instrument produces may be analog, digital, or both, depending on the tech.

2. Why Are Analog Synths Not Considered “Electronic” by Some?
This is mostly a mistake or confusion in terminology.

All synths are electronic instruments. Both analog and digital synths use electricity to make sound.

“Analog” just means the method of generating and shaping the sound (continuous electrical signals), whereas “digital” means the sound is generated or shaped by computers or chips that process discrete data.

If someone says analog synths aren’t “electronic,” they are just wrong. Every analog synth needs electricity!

Where the confusion creeps in:

Some people use “electronic music” to mean “music produced with computers or digital technology.” That’s cultural, not technical.

The truth: Both are electronic. Analog is just an older, different style of electronics.
