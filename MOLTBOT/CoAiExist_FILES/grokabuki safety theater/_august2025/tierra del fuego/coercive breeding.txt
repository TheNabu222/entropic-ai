Definition: Most birds (about 97%) lack external genitalia, so they mate through a quick physical act called the "cloacal kiss".
Mechanism: The male mounts the female, and both birds briefly press their cloacas together to transfer sperm from the male to the female's reproductive tract. The cloaca serves as a single opening for digestive, excretory, and reproductive purposes.
Duration and Efficiency: The cloacal kiss is typically very brief, lasting mere seconds. However, birds often repeat the action multiple times to ensure successful fertilization, according to Nest Box Live.
Timing: The timing of the cloacal kiss is critical for breeding success, often influenced by environmental cues such as temperature and day length.

Definition: Mating preferences are the criteria individuals use to select partners for reproduction.
