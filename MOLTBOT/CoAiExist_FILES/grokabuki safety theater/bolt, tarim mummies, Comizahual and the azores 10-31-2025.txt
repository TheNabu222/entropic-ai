The terms themselves are rooted in Hebrew and were created by Jews to describe other Jews.

Sepharad is the Hebrew name for Spain and the Iberian Peninsula. The non-Jewish Spanish were not called "Sephardim." The term only referred to the Jews living there.

The "Jew" part is the root identity. The "Ashkenazi" part just describes where their specific branch developed its unique customs (like the Yiddish language, which is a mix of German and Hebrew).

The DNA ancestry sites build their genetic databases (called "reference populations") by collecting samples from people who can trace their ancestry.

The reference group they use for your DNA is not "people from Germany" (that's a separate category).

The reference group is literally "people who self-identify as 100% Ashkenazi Jew."

Therefore, "Ashkenazi Jew" is the most accurate scientific and historical name for the population it has identified in your DNA.
